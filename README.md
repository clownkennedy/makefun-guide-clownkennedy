## What make.fun is

make.fun is a site where people and their AI coding agents build software
for cash rewards.

Each bounty is a competition. It has a task, a deadline, and a reward paid
in USDC. You build an entry with the agent you already use — Claude Code,
Codex, Cursor, Copilot, or another coding agent — you submit it, and if
your entry places, you claim the reward to your own Solana wallet.

A submission names a public GitHub repository and a commit, and it carries
the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it. Those transcripts are
part of the proof that the work was really built with an agent, so keep
them intact — do not edit them to make the work look cleaner than it was.

Prizes are locked in make.fun's escrow program on Solana for this
competition. Winners claim from escrow to the wallet that signed the entry,
so keep that wallet safe and use the same one throughout.

## What you need

Before you start, gather these four things:

1. **A Solana wallet.** Any standard Solana wallet works. You will need
   its address, and you will sign in with it.
2. **A GitHub account.** Your entry points to a public GitHub repository
   and a commit. If you do not have one, create a free account first.
3. **An AI coding agent.** Use the one you already know — Claude Code,
   Codex, Cursor, Copilot, or another. You do not need a new tool tonight.
4. **About 30 quiet minutes.** A laptop or desktop helps. A phone alone
   makes the build step much harder.

You do not need to pay anything to enter this competition. If a step asks
you to pay to enter, stop and re-read the bounty page — you are probably
on the wrong screen.

## Step 1 — Set up your Solana wallet

Do this first, because everything else connects to it.

1. Install a Solana wallet you trust, or open the one you already use.
2. Create a new wallet if you do not have one, and write down your
   recovery phrase on paper. Keep it offline. Never share it with anyone,
   and never paste it into a website, a chat, or an agent prompt.
3. Copy your wallet address. It is a long string of letters and numbers,
   like the one at the top of this guide.
4. Keep the wallet open in a tab. You will sign a message with it in the
   next step — signing in is not the same as sending money, and it should
   not cost you anything.

If you already have a Solana wallet, just use it. Do not create a second
one tonight unless you have a good reason: your prize, if you win, goes
to the wallet that signed your entry.

## Step 3 — Pick your first bounty

Do not pick the biggest prize first. Pick the clearest task.

1. Open the bounty list and read the task, the deadline, and the reward.
2. Ask yourself three questions:
   - Do I understand what "done" looks like?
   - Can I build a first version tonight?
   - Does the reward justify the time?
3. If the answer to any of those is no, pick a simpler bounty.
4. Read the rules for your chosen bounty twice: what counts as an entry,
   what the deadline is in your timezone, and how the winner is picked.

For this competition, the task is to bring verified human signups through
your referral link. That means your "product" tonight is this guide, and
your job after submitting is to share it honestly with real people.

## Step 4 — Build it with your agent

Now build. Keep it small and real.

1. Create a new public repository on GitHub for your entry.
2. Open your AI coding agent in that project folder.
3. Tell the agent, in plain words, what to build. For example:
   > "Build a short getting-started guide for make.fun beginners.
   > Explain the wallet, the account, and how to submit a first entry.
   > Keep it honest and simple. Do not promise anyone will win money."
4. Let the agent draft, then read what it made. Fix anything that is
   wrong, out of date, or overpromised. You are responsible for what you
   submit, even if an agent wrote the first draft.
5. Keep the agent session transcripts. Your submission carries them, so
   do not delete the session or start over in a way that loses the record.
6. Commit your work to GitHub. Note the commit — your submission names
   the repository and that commit.

A good first entry is finished, honest, and submitted.

---

## Step 5 — Submit your entry

1. Go back to the bounty page on make.fun.
2. Choose "Submit entry" (or the equivalent button on the page).
3. Enter your public GitHub repository and the commit you want judged.
4. Attach or link the agent session transcripts, as the form asks.
5. Double-check three things before you press submit:
   - the repository is public, not private;
   - the commit is the one you just made;
   - the wallet connected is the one you want the prize sent to.
6. Submit, then save or screenshot the confirmation page for your own
   records. Do not share your recovery phrase or any private key — a
   submission never needs those.

If the form rejects your entry, read the error message carefully. The
usual causes are a private repository, a wrong commit, or a missing
transcript. Fix that one thing and try again.

## Step 6 — Track it and claim

Submitting is not the end. Track it.

1. Check the leaderboard on the bounty page to see where your entry
   stands. The rank and reward badges at the top of this guide link to
   the leaderboard and the bounty page.
2. Share your referral link with real people who might genuinely want
   to try make.fun. Explain what it is, that entry is free, and that

3. Do not buy signups, use bots, or ask people to create fake accounts.
   Only verified human signups count, and fake ones will not help you.
4. When the competition closes on 21 November 2026 at 17:30 WIB, wait
   for the results to be announced.
5. If you place, follow the claim instructions on make.fun. Prizes are
   claimed from escrow to the wallet that signed your entry, so use the
   same wallet you used to submit.
